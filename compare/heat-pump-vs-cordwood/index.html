How the numbers work

Each fuel is converted to delivered heat: cost per million BTU = price ÷ (BTU per unit × system efficiency ÷ 1,000,000). We assume cold-climate heat pump runs at 250% efficiency (a seasonal COP of 2.5) with 3,412 BTU per kWh, and cordwood stove at 65% with 22,000,000 BTU per cord.

With the default prices shown (U.S. average winter 2026-27 forecasts from the EIA where available; see the About page) ($0.18 per kWh for heat pump, $300 per cord for cordwood) and 70 MMBtu of heat, the estimate is $1,477 vs $1,469 per year, favoring cordwood. Your prices will differ, so use your latest bill or supplier quote.

What this does not include

  • Equipment purchase and installation cost, and available rebates or tax credits
  • Maintenance, fan/pump electricity, chimney work, fuel storage
  • Heat pump efficiency drops in very cold weather; the figure used here is a seasonal average
  • Home insulation and size, which change how much heat you need
