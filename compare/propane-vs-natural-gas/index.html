How the numbers work

Each fuel is converted to delivered heat: cost per million BTU = price ÷ (BTU per unit × system efficiency ÷ 1,000,000). We assume propane furnace/boiler runs at 92% efficiency (AFUE-style) with 91,500 BTU per gallon, and natural gas furnace/boiler at 92% with 100,000 BTU per therm.

With the default prices shown (U.S. average winter 2026-27 forecasts from the EIA where available; see the About page) ($2.52 per gallon for propane, $1.3 per therm for natural gas) and 70 MMBtu of heat, the estimate is $2,096 vs $989 per year, favoring natural gas. Your prices will differ, so use your latest bill or supplier quote.

What this does not include

  • Equipment purchase and installation cost, and available rebates or tax credits
  • Maintenance, fan/pump electricity, chimney work, fuel storage
  • Heat pump efficiency drops in very cold weather; the heat pump figure is not used on this page
  • Home insulation and size, which change how much heat you need
