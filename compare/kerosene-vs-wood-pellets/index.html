How the numbers work

Each fuel is converted to delivered heat: cost per million BTU = price ÷ (BTU per unit × system efficiency ÷ 1,000,000). We assume kerosene heater/furnace runs at 80% efficiency (AFUE-style) with 135,000 BTU per gallon, and wood pellet stove at 80% with 16,500,000 BTU per ton.

With the default prices shown (U.S. average winter 2026-27 forecasts from the EIA where available; see the About page) ($4.5 per gallon for kerosene, $330 per ton for wood pellets) and 70 MMBtu of heat, the estimate is $2,917 vs $1,750 per year, favoring wood pellets. Your prices will differ, so use your latest bill or supplier quote.

What this does not include

  • Equipment purchase and installation cost, and available rebates or tax credits
  • Maintenance, fan/pump electricity, chimney work, fuel storage
  • Heat pump efficiency drops in very cold weather; the heat pump figure is not used on this page
  • Home insulation and size, which change how much heat you need
