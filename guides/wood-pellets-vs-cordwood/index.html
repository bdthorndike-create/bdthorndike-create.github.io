Wood Pellets vs Cordwood: Cost Per Unit of Heat

Both are renewable fuels that often undercut oil and propane on price, but they differ a lot in effort, storage and consistency. Cost per MMBtu puts them on the same scale as every other fuel on this site.

How much heat is in a ton or a cord?

At the assumptions used here (about 16.5 million BTU per ton of pellets at 80% stove efficiency, and about 22 million BTU per cord of seasoned hardwood at 65% efficiency), one ton of pellets delivers about as much heat as 112 gallons of heating oil in an 85% boiler, and one cord delivers about as much as 121 gallons. Actual cordwood energy varies by species and moisture, and stove efficiency varies by model and how it is run.

What the numbers don't capture

  • Labor: pellet stoves are fed from bags or a hopper; cordwood means stacking, hauling and tending a fire. If you cut and split your own wood, the cash cost can fall far below the table but the time cost rises.
  • Moisture: wood that is not well seasoned (commonly aimed at under about 20% moisture) delivers less heat and creates more creosote. A moisture meter is inexpensive and worth using.
  • Storage: a cord takes roughly 128 cubic feet of stacked space; pellets are bagged and need to stay dry.
  • Electricity and maintenance: pellet stoves use a small amount of electricity for feed and fans and need regular cleaning; wood stoves and chimneys need annual inspection and sweeping.
  • Safety and rules: install and vent solid-fuel appliances to code, use working smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, and check local wood-burning restrictions.
