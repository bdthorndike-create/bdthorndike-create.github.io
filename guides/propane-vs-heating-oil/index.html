Propane vs Heating Oil: Which Is Cheaper to Heat With?

A gallon of propane holds less energy than a gallon of heating oil, so propane has to sell for noticeably less per gallon to match oil's cost. Comparing the per-gallon price tags alone is the most common mistake.

Why the gallon price misleads

Heating oil carries about 138,500 BTU per gallon and propane about 91,500, so a gallon of oil has roughly 1.5 times the energy. Modern propane furnaces and boilers are often rated slightly more efficient than oil units (this site assumes 92% vs 85%), which narrows the gap but does not close it. With those assumptions, one gallon of oil delivers about 117,700 BTU of heat and one gallon of propane about 84,200 BTU.

Other things that change the answer

  • Equipment: switching fuels means new equipment, a tank, and installation. Running-cost savings have to pay that back.
  • Price volatility and contracts: propane and oil dealers often offer fixed-price or capped-price programs; compare the program price, not the market price.
  • Tank ownership and fees: rental fees, delivery minimums and hazmat or service charges can add real cost.
  • Natural gas availability: where a gas main is available, it is usually far cheaper per unit of heat than either (see the table above), so check before converting between delivered fuels.
