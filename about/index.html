About & Methodology

HeatCostCalc is a free tool for estimating home heating costs. Delivered heat cost = fuel price ÷ (energy content per unit × efficiency). Energy contents: heating oil 138,500 BTU/gal, propane 91,500 BTU/gal, natural gas 100,000 BTU/therm, kerosene 135,000 BTU/gal, electricity 3,412 BTU/kWh, pellets about 16.5 million BTU/ton, seasoned cordwood about 22 million BTU/cord (varies by species). Efficiencies are typical assumptions and can be changed by choosing different prices or load.

Default prices for heating oil ($5.26/gal), propane ($2.52/gal), natural gas ($1.30/therm, converted from $13.44 per thousand cubic feet) and electricity (18 cents/kWh, rounded from the 18.23 cent forecast) come from the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Winter Fuels Outlook 2026-27, published October 6, 2026. They are national averages, so your local price will differ. State pages start with each state's average residential electricity price from the EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A (August 2026); oil, propane and natural gas on those pages still use the national averages above. Defaults for kerosene, wood pellets and cordwood are rough estimates, not sourced figures. Replace every default with your own latest price.
